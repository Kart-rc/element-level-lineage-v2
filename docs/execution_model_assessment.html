Design Review · Lineage Discovery Execution Model

Assessment: one engine, three modes — and the three things it does not cover

The proposal is strong and mostly right. Its best idea — binding the lineage package to the immutable artifact digest — closes a gap your own architecture flags as unsolved. The problems are that a green CI check will assert something it cannot know, that the design silently covers only one of your three substrates, and that the incremental model is unsound as specified.

01 — VERDICT

Adopt — with three design changes and four plan changes

The shape is right. The scope is narrower than it appears, and one label is dangerous.

One portable engine, CI-authoritative, local-optional, LLM behind a governed gateway, enforcement phased from observe to block — every one of those calls is correct, and several are better than what most teams ship. Artifact-digest binding in the release pipeline is the single strongest idea in the document and should be pulled forward, not treated as a late-stage detail: it converts deploy correlation from heuristic inference into a fact.

Three things need to change before this is buildable. First, the PR gate compares the engine's output against a contract the engine itself produced — that is a drift check, not a validation, and calling it "Lineage validation: PASSED" will be read by every engineer as a correctness claim it cannot support. Second, the whole workflow assumes the producer is a repository whose source you analyse; roughly half your estate by data volume emits exact lineage from the engine at execution time and has no place in this pipeline at all. Third, the incremental model as written can miss edges silently, which is the one failure mode a lineage system cannot absorb.

04 — THE MISSING PIECE

The review step is a free labelling pipeline, and the proposal throws it away

This is the highest-value single addition, and it costs almost nothing.

The workflow already has developers reviewing and correcting proposed mappings — that is the point of "require the developer to accept or correct material mappings." Every correction is a labelled ground-truth example produced by the person best qualified to produce it, at the moment they are already looking at the code.

proposal.json          ← what the engine discovered
lineage.yaml (accepted) ← what the developer signed off

  diff(proposal, accepted)
      ↓
  { edge, engine_said, human_said, provenance: DETERMINISTIC|LLM,
    archetype, repo, commit, reviewer, timestamp }
      ↓
  corpus/  ← the calibration set, accumulating for free

Emit that diff as a structured event on every accept. Within a quarter of normal PR traffic you have the labelled corpus that currently blocks confidence calibration, the honest version of the FR-CONF-008 accuracy claim, and any future distillation — without funding a separate labelling project. You also get the two metrics that actually matter and are otherwise unobtainable: correction rate by provenance (how often is the deterministic layer wrong vs the LLM) and correction rate by archetype (which parsers to invest in next).

One design constraint that makes this work: the accepted contract must record which edges a human actually looked at, not just which were committed. A developer who bulk-accepts a 40-edge proposal produces forty labels of value zero, and a corpus polluted with rubber-stamps is worse than a small honest one. Capture an explicit per-edge acknowledgement for material mappings only, and treat everything else as unreviewed.
05 — REVISED MODEL

Three lanes, not three modes

The proposal's table describes Lane A well. Lanes B and C have different authoritative moments, and forcing them through a PR-CI pipeline is the misrouting to avoid.

The cross-lane point. Lane B is not merely another substrate — it is the oracle for Lane A. Where a Spark job reads a topic that a Spring service produces, the plan lineage on the consuming side independently corroborates the inferred lineage on the producing side. That is the only external correctness signal available to Lane A, and it is free. Wire it as a scheduled reconciliation job, not as a CI step.
